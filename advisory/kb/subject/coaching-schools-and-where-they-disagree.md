# The coaching schools

## Why this page exists

An advisory system reading a corpus assembled from four schools' material will, without this page,
produce advice that is a **blend of positions that contradict each other**. Blending is the worst
available option: each school is internally coherent and the average of two coherent methods is
usually neither.

So the schools are named, their disagreements are located, and the corpus holds both sides rather
than resolving them silently.

## The four lineages

| School | Associated with | Core position |
| ------ | --------------- | ------------- |
| **CTS / Koop** | Jason Koop, Jim Rutberg, Corrine Malcolm; the sport's main training text | Evidence-oriented, physiologically framed, comfortable with structured intensity. Treats fuelling and mental skills as first-class disciplines rather than adjuncts |
| **Uphill Athlete / Evoke** | Steve House, Scott Johnston, Kilian Jornet | Large-volume aerobic base first, intensity added late and sparingly. Origin of **Aerobic Deficiency Syndrome** — the claim that athletes overusing high intensity detrain aerobic metabolism, with a "high volume of low- to moderate-intensity training" as the only cure |
| **Maffetone lineage** | Phil Maffetone, the originator of the aerobic-deficiency concept | Heart-rate-capped aerobic training, health-before-performance framing. The intellectual ancestor of the school above, and more absolute |
| **SWAP / Roche** | David and Megan Roche | Adherence and enjoyment as the primary performance variable; consistency and load tolerance over optimisation |

## The three real disagreements

Most of what looks like disagreement between these schools is vocabulary. Three things are
substantive.

### 1. How much intensity, and when

Uphill Athlete and the Maffetone lineage hold that most amateurs are aerobically deficient and that
intensity is the problem, not the solution — the remedy being a long, patient, low-intensity base.
The CTS lineage is more willing to prescribe structured intensity earlier, particularly for athletes
with limited hours.

**Where the evidence sits.** [[polarized-training-review-evidence]] supports low-intensity
*dominance* strongly and does **not** separate specific distributions from one another. So it
supports the shared ground and does not adjudicate the dispute. Both positions are recorded in
[[polarized-intensity-distribution]] and neither is asserted as settled.

**The unresolved sharp point:** the aerobic-base argument is strongest for athletes with many hours
and weakest for the eight-hour amateur, who is the athlete this corpus serves. Nobody has tested it
there.

### 2. Is hiking a discipline or just aerobic work in another posture?

The mountain schools treat steep hiking as a trainable skill with its own economy, worth dedicated
sessions. The running-first schools tend to treat it as low-intensity aerobic work that happens to
be uphill.

This decides whether an athlete's indoor winter should be spent on a stair machine or a treadmill,
and it is the counterpoint recorded in [[vertical-gain-and-its-substitutes]]. There is no evidence
either way in anything consulted.

### 3. Optimisation versus adherence

The SWAP position is that the largest available gain for an amateur is *doing the training at all*,
and that plans optimised past the point of enjoyment lose more to attrition than they gain in
physiology. The other schools do not disagree in principle and do not build around it.

This corpus's own charter leans toward the SWAP reading — see the second falsifier in `purpose.md`
— and that lean is stated there rather than hidden in a page like this one.

## What they agree on, which is more than the argument suggests

- Most training should be easy.
- Consistency over months beats any single block.
- Strength work belongs in the plan.
- Fuelling is trainable and most amateurs under-do it.
- The long run is the load-bearing session.
- Specificity to the actual race matters more as the race approaches — see
  [[periodization-for-ultras]], whose phase *order* is common ground across schools that disagree
  about everything inside the phases. That agreement is itself weak evidence that the order is
  right.

## How to use this page

When two corpus pages conflict, check whether they come from different schools before treating it as
an error. A contradiction traced to a real methodological disagreement is information; the same
contradiction silently averaged is a defect.

The primary texts are queued in [[ultrarunning-book-canon]] and the schools' ongoing output is
mapped in [[ultrarunning-podcasts-blogs-and-newsletters]]. **Every characterisation on this page is
`authority: asserted` and drawn from secondary material** — reading the primary texts is what would
let any of it be stated with more confidence, and until then this page is a map of positions rather
than a judgement between them.
