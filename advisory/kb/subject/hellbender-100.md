# Hellbender 100

## The basics

Starts and finishes at **Camp Grier, Old Fort, North Carolina**. Crosses some of the highest
terrain in the eastern United States, including the summit of **Mount Mitchell** — the highest
peak east of the Mississippi — and drops deep into the valleys between.

| Figure | Value | Source and caveat |
| ------ | ----- | ----------------- |
| Distance | **102.3 mi** | Official course page. Listings also say 100 |
| Surface | ~75.7 mi trail, 15.4 mi gravel, 9.4 mi pavement | Official. Ultrarunning.com disagrees (71 trail / 29.7 gravel) |
| Elevation gain | **~20,000–21,540 ft** | Official says "20,000+"; ATRA says 21,540; Ultrarunning says 21,500+ |
| High point | 5,408 ft (Graybeard) per official; 6,684 ft per ATRA | **These cannot both be right.** Mt Mitchell is 6,684 ft, so the ATRA figure is likely the summit and the official figure a different point |
| Cutoff | **40 hours**, finish 8:00 pm | Official cutoff page lists ten timed aid stations |
| 2027 date | **Fri 7 May 2027** per Ultrarunning.com | The athlete recorded 8 May. A Friday start finishing Saturday evening reconciles both — **confirm** |

**Sample intermediate cutoffs** (from the official page, which may reflect a prior year):
12:45 am at 50.1 mi · 6:30 am at 65.8 mi · 1:00 pm at 83.4 mi.

> **Every figure on this page is `acquired`.** Published sources disagree with each other and the
> official cutoff page may be stale. Before entry and before the taper, re-read the official site
> and update this page. Do not plan a pacing strategy off the table above.

## What the course actually demands

Four demands, in the order they bite:

1. **Sustained climbing, repeatedly.** ~21,000 ft across 102 miles averages roughly 210 ft per
   mile — but it is not distributed evenly; it comes in long, sustained climbs. This is the demand
   a stair machine and an incline treadmill train acceptably well.
2. **Descending, which is the one that ends races.** Roughly the same footage down as up, much of
   it technical, and all of it eccentric quadriceps loading. **No indoor machine trains this.** See
   [[vertical-gain-and-its-substitutes]] — the asymmetry between what indoor training covers and
   what it does not is the central planning problem for anyone who does not live on the course.
3. **A night, and for most of the field most of a second day.** A 40-hour limit with an evening
   finish means a Friday start, one full night out, and finishing in daylight the next day. Mid-
   and back-of-pack runners are making decisions after 24+ hours awake.
4. **Appalachian weather at 6,000 ft in early May.** The summit can be far colder, wetter and
   windier than the valley start. Hypothermia is a live risk on the high sections even when the
   valley is pleasant. Kit is not a formality here.

## The failure chain, specific to this course

The generic ultra failure chain is in [[pacing-by-effort]]. The Hellbender-specific version:

> Big early climbs feel manageable because you are fresh → effort runs above plan on the first
> two ascents → quadriceps take the first long descent already fatigued → descending slows
> sharply from roughly halfway → time banked early is lost with interest → the clock pressure
> arrives at the same time as the night → fuelling degrades under stress and pace → the 50.1-mile
> or 65.8-mile cutoff becomes the binding constraint.

**The intervention is at the first climb, not at the cutoff.** By the time the arithmetic is
tight the decision has been made for several hours.

## Planning consequences for this athlete

- **Descending must be trained deliberately**, and the only places to do it are the mountain
  weekends and the eccentric strength work. This is the strongest argument for protecting the
  monthly mountain trips even when a weekend is inconvenient.
- **Train the night.** At least two long sessions starting at 4 pm or later, and one that runs
  past midnight, before the taper.
- **Walk the climbs on a rule, not a feeling** — a gradient or heart-rate ceiling fixed before
  the race and not renegotiated on the day.
- **Cold kit is mandatory kit.** Test the full high-summit layering on a cold mountain weekend
  in January or February, not for the first time at 5,000 ft at 2 am.
- **The crew holds the stop list.** See [[the-dnf-decision]] — hypothermia and altered
  consciousness are non-negotiable stops, and the person best placed to judge them at hour 28 is
  not the runner.
- **Fuel from hour one** at the practised rate. See [[carbohydrate-fueling-ladder]].
