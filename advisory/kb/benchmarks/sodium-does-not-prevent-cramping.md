# Sodium intake and cramping

> **Medical boundary.** Exercise-associated hyponatremia can be fatal. This page records evidence;
> it does not diagnose or treat, and suspected EAH in the field is a medical emergency.

## The two findings

**1. The null result.** In a field study of ultramarathon runners competing for up to about 30 hours
in hot conditions, **exercise-associated muscle cramping, dehydration, hyponatremia and
nausea/vomiting were unrelated to total sodium intake** — despite a widespread belief among
ultrarunners that sodium prevents these problems. The belief is named in the paper as a belief.

**2. The consensus position.** The Third International Exercise-Associated Hyponatremia Consensus
Development Conference (2015) concluded that the primary aetiology of EAH is **overconsumption of
hypotonic fluid**, in combination with non-osmotic vasopressin secretion, and recommended that
athletes **drink according to thirst — no more, no less**. Drinking beyond thirst is identified as
the primary pathophysiological factor regardless of activity. Prevention is framed as an education
problem.

## Why this page is `reference_role: skeptic`

It exists to make a widely held claim refusable. The claim tracked as
`claim:sodium-prevents-cramp` in `advisory/graph/edges.json` is one of the very few in this corpus
where the popular belief and the evidence point in **opposite** directions, and where acting on the
belief creates the harm: an athlete taking salt to prevent cramp typically drinks more to take it,
and fluid volume is the mechanism of the condition they are most at risk from.

## What the null result does not establish

- It does not show sodium is useless. Palatability, appetite and gastric comfort are real and were
  not the outcomes.
- It does not exclude an effect in **high sweat-sodium individuals**, who were not separately
  identified. This is the strongest form of the commercial counter-argument recorded in
  [[fueling-brands-and-their-interests]], and it is not answered by this study.
- It is a **field** study: sodium intake was observed, not assigned, so confounding by whoever
  chooses to take a lot of salt is unaddressed.

## The practical position

Held in [[drink-to-thirst]]: drink to thirst; treat sodium as palatability and appetite rather than
prophylaxis; and escalate rather than manage when symptoms suggest hyponatremia.
