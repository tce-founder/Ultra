# The polarised training reviews

## What the evidence says

**Review 1 — polarised training and VO2max / work economy.** A systematic review screening 1,836
articles and including 14 found that a polarised approach is **effective for enhancing VO2max,
VO2peak and work economy over the short term**, with the most beneficial pattern being a moderate to
high volume of high-intensity work (15–20%) combined with a substantial volume of low-intensity work
(75–80%).

**Review 2 — polarised versus other distributions.** A systematic review with meta-analysis
comparing polarised training against **other** training intensity distributions found **no reliable
superiority**. This is the finding that matters most and is quoted least.

**Observational baseline.** Well-trained and elite endurance athletes are consistently observed to
train with a large majority of low-intensity work.

**One complicating study.** A comparison of a polarised programme (77% low / 3% moderate / 20% high)
against a "focused endurance" programme (40/50/10) reported **no statistically significant
difference** in improvement, with the focused group achieving similar results in about 17% less
training time.

## The two claims, separated

| Claim | Verdict from this evidence |
| ----- | -------------------------- |
| Most endurance training should be low intensity | **Supported**, observationally and interventionally, consistently |
| *Polarised* specifically beats pyramidal or threshold-focused | **Not supported.** Meta-analysis finds no separation |

This is the split carried into [[polarized-intensity-distribution]], and it is the reason that page
declines to state "80/20" as a finding. It is also the live disagreement between coaching schools
recorded in [[coaching-schools-and-where-they-disagree]].

## Limits that bear directly on this corpus

1. **No ultra population, anywhere.** Every study is of events far shorter than the shortest race
   this corpus is concerned with. The extrapolation may well be sound; it is an extrapolation.
2. **Short intervention windows.** Eight to twelve weeks measures the response of a
   currently-configured athlete, not the shape of a training year.
3. **Volumes are elite.** The studied athletes train two to four times the hours of the amateur this
   corpus serves, and the distribution question at eight hours a week is simply not the question
   these studies asked.
4. **Outcome is VO2max and economy, not performance over distance.** For an ultra, neither is
   obviously the binding constraint.
