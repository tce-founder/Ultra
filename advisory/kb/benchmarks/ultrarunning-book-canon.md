# The ultrarunning book canon

## How to read this page

Every book here is **unread by this corpus**. The page is an **ingest queue**, ordered by what it
would settle, not a set of recommendations. Each row names the specific hole in this vault the book
is expected to fill — because a book that does not close a named gap is a book to enjoy rather than
to ingest, and the distinction is the whole argument of `purpose.md`.

Priority is `advisory/analysis/source-queue.md`; this page is the reference.

## Tier 1 — the training texts

These are the ones whose absence most weakens the corpus. All are `ip_class: licensed` once
distilled.

| Book | Authors | What it is | The gap it closes here |
| ---- | ------- | ---------- | ---------------------- |
| **Training Essentials for Ultrarunning**, 2nd ed. | Jason Koop with Jim Rutberg and Corrine Malcolm | The reference training text for the sport. Reported as ~518 pp, 18 chapters, covering physiology, the disciplines of ultrarunning, mountain training, strength, mental skills, heat and altitude protocols, and race-day nutrition and hydration | **Almost every framework page.** Periodisation is currently assembled from coaching web material with no primary text behind it. This is the highest-value single ingest available |
| **Training for the Uphill Athlete** | Steve House, Scott Johnston, Kilian Jornet (2019) | Mountain-running and ski-mountaineering training from the aerobic-base school. Source of the Aerobic Deficiency Syndrome framing | The vertical-gain and aerobic-base pages, and the *other side* of the intensity-distribution argument in `coaching-schools-and-where-they-disagree` |
| **Training for the New Alpinism** | Steve House, Scott Johnston (2014) | The predecessor text; mountaineering rather than running | Ancestor. Read second, for lineage, not first |
| **Lore of Running** | Tim Noakes | The encyclopaedic reference; the origin of the central-governor argument | Physiology depth, and the primary statement of a model that reaches this corpus only through popular science |
| **80/20 Running** | Matt Fitzgerald | The popular statement of polarised training | Named directly by `polarized-intensity-distribution`. Read it to know what the claim being argued with actually says |

## Tier 2 — mind, pain and effort

| Book | Author | What it is | The gap it closes here |
| ---- | ------ | ---------- | ---------------------- |
| **Endure** | Alex Hutchinson (2018) | Science journalism on the limits of endurance, organised around six limiters — pain, muscle, oxygen, heat, thirst, fuel — and the brain's role in setting them | The mechanism behind [[mental-skills-toolkit]], which currently carries it at second hand |
| **How Bad Do You Want It?** | Matt Fitzgerald | Race narratives read through sport psychology; the psychology of coping strategy | Self-talk and effort perception, with worked examples |
| **Do Hard Things** | Steve Magness | An argument against the toughness-as-trait model, in favour of trainable skills | Directly supports this corpus's split between skills and personality |
| **Peak Performance** | Brad Stulberg, Steve Magness | Stress-plus-rest as a general growth model | The training-block psychology gap named in `mental-skills-toolkit` |
| **The Happy Runner** | David and Megan Roche | Adherence, enjoyment and long-run consistency; explicitly aimed at burnout and boredom | **The motivation and adherence lens**, which is thin here and is the one that decides whether blocks happen |

## Tier 3 — practical guides and memoir

Useful, lower ingest priority. Memoir in particular is `reference_role: example` — it carries
what a race feels like, which nothing else on this page does, and it carries no method.

- **Relentless Forward Progress** — Bryon Powell. The standard practical how-to for a first ultra.
  (Bibliographic details unverified.)
- **Hal Koerner's Field Guide to Ultrarunning** — Hal Koerner. Practical, race-oriented.
  (Unverified.)
- **The Big Book of Endurance Training and Racing** — Phil Maffetone. The primary statement of the
  aerobic-deficiency lineage that reaches this corpus through Uphill Athlete. Read for provenance.
- **Eat and Run** / **North** — Scott Jurek. Memoir.
- **Born to Run** — Christopher McDougall. Journalism and myth-making; historically important to the
  sport's growth and unreliable as method.
- **The Brave Athlete** — Simon Marshall and Lesley Paterson. Applied sport psychology with
  exercises. (Existence and contents unverified in this research pass — queued, not recommended.)
- **Sport Nutrition** — Jeukendrup and Gleeson. A textbook rather than a trade book; the underlying
  reference for most of the fuelling material in the sport.

## What this page does not contain, deliberately

**A ranking.** Ordering by "best" invites reading the first one and stopping. The ordering here is by
*what this corpus lacks*, which is a different question and the only one that should drive an ingest.

**Anything about a book nobody has opened, beyond what it claims to cover.** Descriptions here come
from publisher copy, retailer listings and reader summaries. That is why `citation_status:
recommended` and `authority: asserted`. When a book is read, it gets its own page, the frontmatter
moves to `summarized`, and this row shrinks to a pointer.

The complementary non-book resources are in [[ultrarunning-podcasts-blogs-and-newsletters]], and the
research literature is mapped in [[coaching-schools-and-where-they-disagree]] for practice and in
`where-the-evidence-lives` for evidence.
