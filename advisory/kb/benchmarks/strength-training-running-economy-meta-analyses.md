# Strength training and running economy

## The findings

| Finding | Detail |
| ------- | ------ |
| **Effect exists** | Strength training improves running economy in middle- and long-distance runners |
| **Magnitude** | Roughly **3–5%** improvement in submaximal running economy |
| **Method matters** | **High-load** and **combined** (heavy + plyometric) formats produced a small significant improvement. **Submaximal-load** and **isometric-only** did not |
| **Frequency matters** | **2–3 sessions per week** outperformed one |
| **Progression matters** | Progressive resistance exceeded static-load approaches |
| **Speed dependence** | The magnitude of the effect depends on the speed at which economy is assessed |

That last row is the one this corpus should worry about most, and it is stated in the source
literature rather than being our inference: **the effect size varies with the test speed.** Every
test speed in this evidence base is far above the pace of an ultra.

## The null result worth keeping

Submaximal-load and isometric formats showed **no significant improvement**. That is the evidential
basis for the position in [[strength-training-for-ultrarunners]] that high-rep bodyweight circuits —
the format most commonly prescribed to runners — are not merely less effective but were not shown to
work at all in this outcome. A null result is easy to leave out of a summary and it is doing real
work here.

## What is being extrapolated, and by whom

The argument that economy gains matter *more* over ultra distances (a fixed percentage saving
compounds over a larger energy budget) is a mechanistic inference. It is reasonable. It is not in
these papers. In [[strength-training-for-ultrarunners]] it is carried at `asserted`, deliberately,
and the frequency and load prescriptions are carried at `established` — the two must not be quoted
with the same confidence.

Likewise "economy under fatigue" or "durability": plausible, mechanistically motivated, and not what
was measured, because every measurement was in a rested athlete.
