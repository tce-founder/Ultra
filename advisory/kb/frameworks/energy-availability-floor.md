# The energy availability floor

> **Medical boundary.** Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs) is a clinical syndrome with
> endocrine, skeletal, cardiovascular, immune and psychological consequences. This page exists to
> make an advisory system stop and refer, not to diagnose. Suspected REDs, disordered eating,
> menstrual dysfunction, or a stress fracture goes to a sports physician and, where indicated, to a
> dietitian and a psychologist. Nothing here is a substitute for that and no advisory output may
> imply otherwise.

## What question it answers

*Before any training question — is this athlete eating enough to be trained at all?*

## Why it sits under everything else

REDs describes impaired health and performance arising from **low energy availability relative to the
demands of training** — not from a low weight, and not necessarily from an eating disorder. Affected
systems include energy metabolism, reproductive function, bone health, immune function, protein
synthesis and cardiovascular health. Endurance athletes are named as a higher-risk group, and low
energy availability is common in competitively trained men as well as women.

The practical consequence for an advisory system is a **precedence rule**: an athlete below the
energy availability floor cannot be advised on periodisation, intensity distribution or fuelling
rate, because every one of those recommendations assumes adaptation will occur, and under-fuelling
is precisely the state in which it does not. Training advice given to an under-fuelled athlete does
not merely fail to work — it accelerates the harm.

**So this is a gate, not a topic.** It runs before the training lenses, not alongside them.

## Inputs

- Training load, honestly counted.
- Any of the signal set below.
- Willingness to involve a clinician. If that is absent, the advisory output is that and nothing
  else.

## The signals that should stop a training conversation

None of these is diagnostic; each is a reason to refer. The list is deliberately behavioural and
observable rather than biochemical, because those are what an advisory system can actually see:

- Loss of menstrual periods, or a change in cycle regularity, in an athlete not on hormonal
  contraception
- A bone stress injury — one is a signal, two is a pattern
- Persistent fatigue that recovery weeks do not touch, plus falling performance at unchanged load
- Recurrent illness, poor wound healing, feeling cold constantly
- Deliberate weight loss during a build, or weight loss the athlete did not intend
- Low mood, irritability, loss of libido, disturbed sleep alongside the above
- Any rigidity around food, any food group eliminated without a clinical reason, any anxiety about
  eating that the athlete would not want described out loud

## Steps

1. **Ask the gate question first**, at the start of a block and at every review: is intake matching
   load, and is any of the signal set present?
2. **If yes, stop and refer.** Do not proceed to the training question. Record the referral in the
   action register.
3. **If no, fuel the training rather than fuelling the day.** The commonest sub-clinical version of
   this problem is not restriction but *inadvertence*: hard sessions on top of a normal day's food,
   repeated. See [[carbohydrate-fueling-ladder]] for the in-session half of the same problem.
4. **Do not manipulate body composition during a build.** If it is to be attempted at all it belongs
   in an off-season, with professional support, and it is out of scope here.
5. **Prefer training less to training under-fuelled.** This is the least intuitive consequence and
   the most important one: an athlete who cannot fuel ten hours a week should train eight — see
   [[minimum-effective-dose-and-time]].

## Output

A recorded answer to the gate question with a date, and either a clear pass or a referral. Not a
score.

## How to tell it is being applied wrongly

- **It is treated as a women's issue.** Prevalence work in male endurance athletes says otherwise,
  and the belief is itself a reason male athletes go unassessed.
- **A number is computed and trusted.** EA arithmetic inherits the error of a body-composition
  estimate; the signal set above is more reliable than the calculation.
- **It is raised only when performance drops.** By then the bone has been demineralising for a while.
- **The advisory system diagnoses.** The output is a referral, always. The evidence base and its
  limits are recorded in [[reds-and-low-energy-availability-evidence]].
