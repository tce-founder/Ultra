# Mental skills

## What question it answers

*Which of the mental side of this is trainable, and what is the actual training?*

## The distinction the sport keeps failing to make

Ultrarunning talks about toughness as a trait. The useful material is about **skills**, and skills
have protocols. The separation matters because trait-framing produces no action: an athlete told
they need to be tougher has been given nothing to do on Tuesday.

Four things are trainable and have some evidential support:

| Skill | What it is | Standing |
| ----- | ---------- | -------- |
| **Self-talk** | Deliberately replacing spontaneous negative statements with prepared instructional or motivational ones | Reasonable evidence in shorter endurance protocols; `established` there, `asserted` at ultra duration |
| **Chunking / segmentation** | Reducing the object of attention from the whole race to the next aid station, climb, or ten minutes | `experienced`; near-universal in practitioner accounts, little formal evidence |
| **Reframing the meaning of pain** | Treating discomfort as information about effort rather than as a signal of damage | `established` as a mechanism (effort perception is centrally modulated), `asserted` as a protocol |
| **Attentional strategy** | Choosing association (monitoring the body) or dissociation (attention outward), deliberately and by phase | `established` that both exist and differ; `asserted` which to use when |

And two things that are commonly offered as skills and are closer to circumstance: natural pain
tolerance, and "wanting it more". Neither has a protocol, and framing them as the answer is what
sends athletes to buy another book instead of practising.

## The mechanism worth understanding

The central claim of the modern endurance-psychology literature is that the limit reached in a long
effort is regulated centrally — the brain's model of what remains possible — rather than being a
hard peripheral ceiling. Fatigue is a perception the brain constructs, and perceptions are
manipulable: by self-talk, by expectation, by knowing the distance remaining, by facial expression,
by caffeine, by other people.

The honest version: this is well supported as a *mechanism* and thinly supported as a *magnitude*.
It does not mean the limits are imaginary. It means they are further out than they feel and closer
than optimism suggests.

## Steps

1. **Write the self-talk in advance, in the athlete's own words.** Prepared phrases, tied to
   specific predicted moments: the low at hour six, the first climb after dark, the point where the
   fuelling plan has to restart. Generic affirmations do not survive contact.
2. **Rehearse in training, in the state.** A skill practised only when fresh is not available when
   tired. The last hour of the long run is the laboratory.
3. **Chunk to the next decision, not to the finish.** The unit is an aid station or a climb.
4. **Practise the reframe explicitly.** Naming the sensation — "this is effort, my legs work, I ate
   forty minutes ago" — is the operation. It is also a diagnostic: if the checks fail, the problem is
   physical and the answer is in [[the-dnf-decision]].
5. **Use the pre-race routine as a physiological intervention too.** Pre-race anxiety correlates
   with GI symptoms; a settled start line is a fuelling measure as much as a mental one.
6. **Externalise the hard decisions.** The crew holds the DNF list precisely because these skills
   degrade with sleep loss.

## Output

A one-page race-day mental plan: predicted low points, prepared statements for each, the chunking
unit, and what gets checked before concluding anything.

## How to tell it is being applied wrongly

- **It is read rather than practised.** The books in [[ultrarunning-book-canon]] are worth reading
  and reading is not the intervention.
- **The reframe is used to override a real signal.** Pain that changes gait is not a perception to
  be managed.
- **Toughness is the diagnosis.** It generates no action. Almost every "mental" failure in an
  amateur ultra is a fuelling, pacing, sleep or preparation failure presenting as a mood.
- **It is applied to the race and not to the block.** The mental problem that ends more seasons is
  the Tuesday in February, and that is a different subject — see
  [[minimum-effective-dose-and-time]].
