# The DNF decision

## What question it answers

*Should I stop?* — asked by the person least equipped to answer it, at the worst hour, alone.

## The structural problem

Every element of the decision is compromised at exactly the moment it is made. The athlete is
sleep-deprived, and sleep deprivation degrades judgement before it degrades legs (see
[[sleep-and-the-night]]). They are frequently under-fuelled, which degrades mood specifically. They
are in pain, which is not the same as being damaged, and they cannot easily tell the difference. And
the two failure modes are symmetric and both real: **stopping a race that could have been finished**,
and **continuing one that should have been stopped**.

So the decision is not improved by thinking harder in the moment. It is improved by having decided
most of it in advance, in a state where the deciding worked.

## The three categories, decided beforehand

| Category | Examples | Rule |
| -------- | -------- | ---- |
| **Stop, no negotiation** | Suspected hyponatremia, chest pain, altered consciousness, suspected bone stress injury, hypothermia not reversing with the kit carried, any injury that changes gait | Non-negotiable, written down, held by the crew. Not the athlete's call in the moment |
| **Solve, then reassess** | Nausea, no energy, cold, dark thoughts, blisters, "this is stupid and I hate it" | **Every one of these has a countermeasure and a timebox.** Nothing in this row is a reason to stop until the countermeasure has been tried for the stated time |
| **Genuine judgement** | Cutoff arithmetic no longer works; the goal that made the race worth doing is gone; conditions have changed materially | Decide with the crew, at an aid station, sitting down, after eating |

Most DNFs that are later regretted come from row two being treated as row one. Almost all of row two
is fixable, and the fix takes twenty to forty minutes: eat, drink, sit, change socks, put on a
jacket, walk, wait for it to be light.

## Inputs

- The category table above, written before the race and given to the crew.
- The cutoff arithmetic — the actual numbers, on paper, so the third row is arithmetic rather than
  a feeling.
- A named person authorised to hold the non-negotiable list.

## Steps

1. **Write the three lists before the race**, when rested. This is the entire intervention.
2. **Never DNF at night, and never between aid stations.** Both are rules about state, not about
   toughness. The dark hours produce more quitting than the same conditions in daylight, and a
   decision made walking alone has had no food, no chair and no other voice in it.
3. **Timebox row two.** "I will eat, sit for twenty minutes, and reassess" resolves a large fraction
   of what presents as a decision to stop.
4. **Say it out loud to a person.** Articulating the reason converts a mood into a claim, and most
   moods do not survive being stated as claims. This is a mental-skills technique — see
   [[mental-skills-toolkit]].
5. **When it is row one, stop immediately and without debate.** The list exists so this is fast.
6. **Record the reason afterwards, honestly, within a day.** The reason will be rewritten by memory
   within a week, in whichever direction is more comfortable, and the record is what the next race
   plan is built from.

## Output

Three written lists held by the crew, cutoff arithmetic on paper, and a post-race honest record.

## How to tell it is being applied wrongly

- **The plan is "never quit".** That is not a decision rule, it is the removal of one, and it is how
  people finish races with stress fractures.
- **Row-two problems get row-one treatment.** The commonest regret.
- **The crew has not been told.** A crew that does not know the list will support whatever the
  athlete says at 4 a.m., which is the failure mode.
- **The DNF is treated as a character verdict.** It is data about a plan. The chain that most often
  produces it — pacing, then fuelling, then judgement — is traced in [[pacing-by-effort]], and the
  withdrawal figures are in [[gi-distress-and-dnf-figures]]. Each link is a fixable planning error
  and none of them is a fact about the person.
